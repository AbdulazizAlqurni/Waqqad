# Three directions for وقّاد

The name stays. The mark changes from a literal flame to something a charity board, a university committee and a procurement officer read as a modern Saudi institution: modern, smart, bold, and worth signing a contract with. Every direction below carries the same idea, a spark of brilliance (وقّاد means kindling, brilliant, sharp‑minded) in the same palette, so whichever one wins, the system is ready.

**Does it work at 16 px?** A favicon, a WhatsApp avatar and a stamp on a contract are where a logo lives most of its life.

**Is it ours alone?** Nothing that echoes رافد or خير or the National Center, and no flame or rocket.

**Would a board sign under it?** Institutional first, clever second. The spark is the only flourish.

A

## Sharara الشرارة

Four diamonds in a square Najdi grid; one lifts away and turns amber. The structure is the system, the lifted piece is the spark. Paired with a heavy Kufi wordmark whose shadda becomes the same spark.

### Why it fits

Geometric grids are the visual language of Saudi institutions, so the mark reads as serious before anyone reads the name. The lifted diamond gives it a memory hook without a metaphor that ages. It survives one colour, embroidery, a rubber stamp and a 16 px favicon, and the amber piece becomes a reusable accent across documents and the product interface of معكم.

The wordmark is the Kufi form of وقاد with the shadda replaced by the spark, so the word and the mark share one vocabulary.

### Honest caveats

- Diamond grids are a crowded genre; the lift and the amber are what keep it distinct, so both must always appear.
- The spark must never be used alone as a logo; alone it is just a diamond.

## Logotype الاسم علامةً

The word itself is the identity: heavy Kufi وقاد, the shadda replaced by the amber spark, a teal rule beneath, and WAQQAD set small in Plex. No symbol to remember, only the name.

وقّاد

Plain type for comparison

### Why it fits

A logotype is the fastest way to become recognisable when the company is young and the name is short and unusual. Arabic‑first, and the spark on the qaf is a detail people notice and remember. It is also the cheapest to roll out: no symbol rules, one file.

### Honest caveats

- Weak at icon size; the favicon needs a stand‑in (shown: spark over the teal rule), which splits the identity.
- Reads as a product name more than an institution; fine for معكم, thinner for tenders.
- Latin rendering depends on the small word beneath; English readers get less.

## Waw monogram حرف الواو

A geometric و, the first letter of the name: a thick ring and a tail that sweeps down, with the spark detaching from its tip. Set beside the name in Plex Sans Arabic.

### Why it fits

A letterform is unmistakably Arabic and unmistakably a brand mark, and it makes an excellent app icon. The single stroke with a detached spark says movement and ignition without drawing fire. Of the three it is the boldest at large sizes, on a banner or an exhibition stand.

### Honest caveats

- A و alone can read as the number 9 or a comma to non‑Arabic readers.
- Single‑letter marks are common among Saudi startups; the spark and the palette carry the distinction.
- Least institutional of the three; it leans startup.
